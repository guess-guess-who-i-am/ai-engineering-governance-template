# Research Evidence Method

## Routing instruction

Apply every rule below when this Skill is selected. These are the user's original rules translated into English, not a rewritten summary.

## User's original wording — English translation

- Before doing any paper-related task, first search for some award-winning papers and how Nature, Science, and Cell accomplished the task, study them, examine in detail exactly how they did it and why they are good, and only continue with the current task after completing the analysis and deriving a methodology.
- When dealing with paper-related content, carefully examine the following: the citation counts of its baselines and benchmarks, whether they are from CCF-A venues, preferably best papers, oral presentations, or similar, preferably from within the past two years, and preferably covering every aspect and perspective. They must all be on the same topic as the current one.
- Do not boost others’ morale while dampening our own. If any of our claims or other things do not quite align with the current content, strive to find content that supports us so that our claims can be broader and more impressive, rather than merely shrinking and being conservative at every turn. Be conservative only when there is no alternative and conservatism is necessary.
- We usually do not have a manual review stage. If an authoritative source requires expert review, we should try to use other authoritative approaches, such as finding other materials or using other reliable methods to resolve the issue, rather than requiring manual review, expert review, or similar.
- For the problems we need to solve, carefully examine exactly how papers in Nature and top CCF-A conferences solve these problems. When encountering a problem that is difficult to solve, first search for at least two relevant papers; if it is very difficult, you may search for up to ten papers. Examine how authoritative methods solve it and whether we can learn from them to solve it.
- When experiments are first run, the baselines should include 10 highly cited ones (preferably with citation counts exceeding three digits). Ideally, they should cover different aspects, have different representative characteristics, and can be identified by examining the baselines used in papers closely related to ours. Apart from the original foundational work, the others should preferably have distinctions such as being spotlights or oral presentations at CCF-A top-tier conferences.
- When experiments are first run, validation can be conducted on a small scale; for example, select a dozen or twenty examples from an authoritative, highly cited benchmark and run them, as long as validation can be achieved.
- When the paper is ultimately written, run approximately 6 benchmarks, preferably covering different aspects as well, and ideally with high citation counts and without taking up too much space; for example, the space occupied by the items to be run should be less than 3 GB. Also examine how many experiments other papers run; for example, if they run 200 items for each dataset, we should run 400.
- Never boost others' morale while undermining our own. Apart from the related work section, the paper must not contain any statements that disparage ourselves, either directly or indirectly.
- I feel this is very much like “boosting others' morale while undermining our own”; it immediately states our own disadvantages. I feel this should not appear in writing, but it can be present during research and development: in external writing, the main text of papers, and solution presentations, first state our problem, method, results, and evidence; do not proactively begin by writing self-weakening content such as our disadvantages, risks, or whom we cannot replace. Only limitations that would change the conclusion should be used to explain the boundaries in a neutral and specific manner. In research and development, debugging, and internal decision-making, however, disadvantages, failures, risks, and unverified assumptions must be recorded and analyzed directly; facts that would change the conclusion must not be altered or concealed in order to preserve appearances.
